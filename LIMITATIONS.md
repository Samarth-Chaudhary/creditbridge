# CreditBridge: Architectural, Statistical & Operational Limitations

**Document Status**: Formal Model Risk Management (MRM) Disclosure  
**Governance Requirement**: Supervisory Guidance on Model Risk Management (SR 11-7 / OCC 2011-12) & RBI Digital Lending Guidelines  

---

## 1. Executive Disclosure: Prototype Framing

CreditBridge is an alternative-credit underwriting **research and engineering prototype**, NOT a commercially validated lending model or credit bureau replacement. It is strictly prohibited from autonomous loan origination or automated credit approval.

Every commercial financial institution deploying alternative credit underwriting must understand the structural boundaries and unmodeled failure modes detailed below.

---

## 2. Statistical & Data Limitations

### 2.1 Complete Reliance on Synthetic Training Data
- **The Reality**: The model was trained entirely on simulated data (`data/synthetic_borrowers.csv` and `data/temporal_synthetic_borrowers.csv`). While distributions were parameterized against published Indian fintech studies (UPI transaction frequencies, recharge amounts, gig platform ratings), no real customer repayment records were used in model fitting.
- **Consequence**: The baseline ROC-AUC of **0.6240** and KS of **21.08%** reflect the model's ability to separate synthetic latent risk equations, not human repayment behavior under economic stress. Real-world performance could degrade unpredictably.

### 2.2 Lack of Macroeconomic & Systemic Shocks
- **The Reality**: Synthetic generation models stable idiosyncratic borrower dynamics. It does not simulate macro shocks such as:
  - Regulatory interventions (e.g., changes to UPI interchange fees or RBI unsecured lending risk weights).
  - Platform policy shocks (e.g., aggregator algorithm changes reducing gig delivery partner daily order volumes by 40%).
  - Regional climate events (e.g., monsoon disruptions halting daily wage construction labor).
- **Consequence**: In periods of systemic economic stress, default correlations spike across all informal borrower segments simultaneously, invalidating portfolio loss projections.

### 2.3 Single Statement Boundary & Adverse Account Selection
- **The Reality**: In the real-world ingestion mode, CreditBridge parses individual uploaded CSV bank/UPI statements.
- **The Attack / Gaming Vector**: Thin-file borrowers frequently maintain multiple digital wallets or accounts across multiple banks. A financially distressed borrower can selectively upload their cleanest statement (showing gig earnings) while concealing secondary accounts carrying defaulted informal loans or high-cost borrowing.
- **Production Solution Required**: Commercial deployment cannot rely on user-uploaded documents; it requires automated, multi-account data aggregation via the **RBI Account Aggregator (AA) ecosystem** to capture consolidated liabilities.

## 3. Machine Learning & Behavioral Gaming Vulnerabilities

### 3.1 Proxy Metric Manipulation (Goodhart's Law)
- **The Vulnerability**: When a proxy measure becomes the target criterion for credit approval, it ceases to be a good measure.
  - *UPI Velocity Gaming*: A borrower can artificially inflate `monthly_upi_transaction_count` and `income_stability_index` by conducting circular, zero-fee peer-to-peer (P2P) transfers with friends or family.
  - *Utility Timing*: Borrowers can schedule automated micro-payments to simulate consistent utility payment discipline.
- **Current Mitigation**: The `transaction_classifier.py` flags zero-sum self-transfers, round-trip reversals, and circular transfers. However, determined adversarial rings can defeat rule-based heuristic filters without centralized graph-based fraud monitoring.

### 3.2 Modest Baseline Predictive Power (AUC 0.6240)
- **The Reality**: A ROC-AUC of 0.6240 and KS of 21.08% represents a modest discriminator (conventional prime banking models achieve 0.75+ AUC).
- **Consequence**: The false positive rate (classifying a non-defaulting borrower as high-risk) and false negative rate (approving a borrower who ultimately defaults) are significant. The model cannot support aggressive automated balance sheet expansion without substantial loss reserves.

### 3.3 Explainability Disclosures: Attribution vs. Causality
- **The Reality**: Feature importance from SHAP or logistic regression coefficients represents **statistical model attribution**, NOT causal economic truth.
- **Underwriter Caution**: Informing a declined borrower that "increasing mobile recharge regularity by 15% will result in loan approval" is factually incorrect and misleading. Recharge regularity correlates with disposable liquidity but does not cause creditworthiness. All adverse action notices generated by CreditBridge are explicitly labeled as model attribution.

## 4. Algorithmic Fairness & Kleinberg Impossibility Bounds

### 4.1 Inherent Mathematical Tradeoffs
Per Kleinberg et al. (2016) and Chouldechova (2017), when base default rates differ across demographic groups (e.g., 20.9% for daily wage labor vs 10.6% for gig rideshare), it is mathematically impossible for an underwriting model to simultaneously satisfy:
1. **Demographic Parity** (Equal approval rates across groups)
2. **Equal Opportunity / Predictive Parity** (Equal true positive rates)
3. **Calibration within Groups** (Predicted default probabilities equal observed default rates across groups)

### 4.2 CreditBridge Strategic Choice
CreditBridge explicitly prioritizes **Probability Calibration** and accurate Expected Loss calculation over artificial Demographic Parity. Forcing equal approval rates across groups with vastly different baseline cashflow volatility would require approving high-risk borrowers into unaffordable debt traps, violating responsible lending mandates.

## 5. Security & Operational Limitations

### 5.1 Python Pickle Deserialization Risk (CWE-502)
- **The Reality**: The model weights are serialized using Python's standard `pickle` / `joblib` formats. Python pickles are execution engines, not passive data stores.
- **Mitigation**: Deserialization is protected by strict SHA-256 integrity verification (`bfbabaa4...`). In an enterprise banking deployment, models should be converted to ONNX or strict JSON/PMML formats to eliminate arbitrary execution surfaces entirely.

### 5.2 Heuristic Rule Fragility in Bank Statement Parsing
- **The Reality**: Indian banks do not adhere to a single standardized CSV structure. Bank statement formats frequently change column headers, narration layouts, or date stamps without advance notification.
- **Mitigation**: The canonical parser handles 6 major institutional formats and falls back to regex date disambiguation. However, unmapped bank exports may fail to parse or require manual schema mapping.
